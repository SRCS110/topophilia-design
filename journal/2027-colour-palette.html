The 2027 Palette: Greens, Warm Earth and the Quiet End of Beige

For once, the major paint houses mostly agree. 2027 is green, and the rooms that wear it best start with something you already own.

November 22, 2026 · 3 min read

The Builder

Each autumn, paint companies announce the colour they believe will shape the year ahead. Usually they scatter: a blue here, a terracotta there. This year, they've landed in nearly the same field.

A year of green

  • Behr: Grounded, a mossy olive with brown in it.
  • Sherwin-Williams: Celery, a pale, fresh green.
  • Benjamin Moore: Webster Green HC-130, a deeper, historic green.
  • Dulux: Soulful Sage, a light sage with soft grey undertones.

Around them sit a few supporting voices. Valspar chose Cottage Door, a powdery blue that sits between warm and cool. Graham & Brown chose Rose Earth, a dusty, grounded pink. Sherwin-Williams' wider forecast leans into ochre, clay, deep blue and creamy white.

Read together, the message is clear: cool greys and flat beiges are on their way out. In their place come colours that feel pulled from a landscape: moss, olive, sage, clay, mushroom, sky.

Why it's happening

Forecasters describe a pull away from the perfect, digitally smoothed spaces of the last few years. People want rooms that feel warm, grounded and human. Pale greens are increasingly treated as the new neutral. They read as calm the way white does, but they hold more warmth and change beautifully through the day.

For those of us in Northern California, this palette feels less like a trend than a description of home: olive groves, oak-covered hills, vineyard rows in late summer, fog-grey mornings. It is a palette that belongs here.

Build the plan from something you love

The most useful idea in this year's colour conversation doesn't come from a paint chart at all. Designers are increasingly pulling a room's palette from an object: an antique rug, a piece of pottery, a painting, a faded quilt. It's how we like to work. Find the thing in the room you'd save in a fire, and let it set the colours.

From there, a few principles carry a plan from one room to the next:

  • Layer tones, don't just pick one. 2027 rooms often use several shades of the same colour, with a lighter wall, a deeper trim and a darker ceiling, for a wrapped, cocooning feel.
  • Paint the storage, not a feature wall. Built-ins, bookshelves and alcoves in a stronger shade are replacing the single accent wall.
  • Keep jewel tones for accents. Aubergine, deep red and ink blue work best on upholstery, curtains and ceramics, where they can be changed.
  • Let wood do some of the work. Walnut, oak and aged pine are colours too, and warm greens are kind to all of them.

A cohesive home, not a series of rooms

A good colour plan is less about any single shade and more about how one room hands off to the next. When the hall's olive picks up the sage of the kitchen, and the bedroom's dusty pink echoes a rug downstairs, a home starts to feel like one place rather than a set of separate decisions.
